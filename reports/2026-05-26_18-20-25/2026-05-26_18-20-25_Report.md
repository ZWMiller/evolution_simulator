# Stable-matching mating drives 10x population growth, but speciation machinery decouples from habitat adaptation

---

## Experiment Summary

**Config:** `ring_stable_30k.toml` (copy in this folder)
**Log dir:** `simulation_logs/2026-05-26_18-20-25/`
**Run date:** 2026-05-26

First long run using the new `stable_matching` mating strategy (Gale-Shapley deferred acceptance)
on a 5-habitat ring topology, replacing the legacy `zip` pairing that suffered from a severe
minority-species Allee effect. Primary goals: confirm that stable_matching sustains multi-species
coexistence across habitats, and observe whether allopatric speciation arises from spontaneous
route isolation.

**Key non-defaults:**

| Parameter | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `mating_strategy` | `stable_matching` | First full test of new mating system |
| `weeks` | 30,000 | ~750–1,200 generations; long enough to observe speciation chains |
| `creatures_per_species` | 60 | Larger founding population to buffer early extinction |
| `founding_habitat_bias` | 0.55 | Partial adaptation to prevent early-generation extinction |
| `isolation_probability` | 1e-5 | Target ~1.5 route closures over run (5 links × 30k weeks) |
| `stats_every` / `events_every` | 50 / 0 | Speed; no per-individual event log |
| `initial_species_per_habitat` | 3 | 15 founding species total across 5 habitats |

**Starting state:** 891 creatures, 15 founding species across 5 habitats in a ring
(Desert → Savanna → Forest → Tundra → Alpine → Desert).
Founding species: silent prowler, hungry nomad, hooded fisher, wild weaver, violent sifter,
twisted hooker, dark blazer, gleaming piercer, waning prowler, glowing seeker, iron stalker,
venomous wriggler, northern drifter, slow carver, distant forager.

## Report Summary

**Why this was interesting:** First long run with stable_matching; also the first run long enough
to clearly reveal the multi-generational dynamics of speciation and adaptation in a connected
ring topology.

**What was found:**
- **Stable_matching produced ~10x the carrying capacity** of equivalent zip runs — population
  grew from 891 → 8,876 across 30,000 weeks, with no extinction.
- **Species-level diversity oscillated in a narrow band** (~20–37 living species at any given
  snapshot) despite 2,034 total species ever named — continuous rapid turnover, not coexistence.
- **Speciation expressed as directional selection chains**, not ecological divergence: each
  dominant species was progressively replaced by a descendant that peeled off its most-adapted
  members via cladogenesis. The population is evolving, but the progress logs as species
  replacement rather than in-place change.
- **Evolution discovered a metabolic tolerance strategy** (metabolism −0.42, water_efficiency +0.08,
  fecundity +1.09) that outcompeted habitat alignment across 1,200 generations — food prob fell
  from 0.877 to 0.573 while population grew. This reveals a genuine alternative fitness optimum
  in the cost surface, not a simulation failure; the cost parameters need rebalancing so both
  strategies remain viable rather than tolerance fully displacing alignment pressure.
- **Zero route isolation events**: `isolation_probability = 1e-5` was too low — no allopatric
  speciation from geographic isolation occurred despite the target of ~1.5 events.
- **Zero anagenesis events**: phenotype drift never hit the 0.93 threshold with 60-week
  persistence, even over 1,200 generations.
- **k-means split species tend to burn out rapidly**: the split threshold equals the mating
  floor (both 0.70), creating leaky boundaries where hybrids from the parent species dilute
  the smaller split-off cluster back into the parent.

**Takeaway:** Stable_matching is a clear improvement for population viability, but the simulation
is revealing two deeper tensions: (1) the speciation mechanism operates in compatibility-gene
space, which is partially decoupled from the habitat-adaptation axis — allowing evolution to
"escape" habitat pressure by becoming resource-tolerant instead of resource-aligned; and (2)
carrying capacity is growing without bound because the density penalty is too weak relative to
the reproductive boost from stable_matching. Both need to be addressed before results are
ecologically meaningful.

## Report

### Population growth and carrying capacity

The population grew from 891 (week 1) to 8,876 (week 30,000), nearly 10x:

| Week | Total pop | Living species |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 891 | 15 |
| 1,000 | 4,081 | 27 |
| 5,000 | 4,616 | 30 |
| 10,000 | 4,976 | 37 |
| 15,000 | 6,350 | 28 |
| 20,000 | 6,948 | 26 |
| 25,000 | 8,023 | 33 |
| 30,000 | 8,876 | 21 |

This growth is a direct consequence of the mating strategy change. Under `zip`, every
cross-species pairing wasted both individuals' mating slot — a severe Allee tax on minority
species that also capped total reproductive output. Under `stable_matching`, every viable pair
that wants to mate gets to mate. The result is a much higher effective birth rate at equivalent
population size, and the carrying capacity is never reached because the density-dependent
predation penalty (`PREDATION_ALPHA · N / POPULATION_SUPPORT`) is too weak to fully offset
the gain. **The density predation term needs to be meaningfully harsher for future runs.**

Per-habitat final populations were uneven: Eastern Savanna (2,592) and Southern Forest (2,195)
dominated, while Western Tundra (1,062) and Central Alpine (936) remained constrained — likely
reflecting those habitats' harsher resource constants rather than connectivity differences.

### Species turnover: directional selection expressed as cladogenesis chains

At every weekly snapshot, only 20–37 species were alive — remarkably stable given that 2,034
total species were named over the run. The species pool underwent complete turnover multiple
times. None of the 15 founding species survived to week 30,000.

The dominant pattern in the trait comparison visualizer is "spike → plateau → death spiral":
each species grows rapidly after appearance, holds for some span of weeks, then declines to
extinction. This is not random demographic noise. The mechanism is:

1. A large, successful species accumulates members. Its most-adapted individuals drift furthest
   from the species' compatibility centroid.
2. Those leading-edge individuals accumulate as a candidate cluster for 60+ weeks.
3. They are promoted to a new species (cladogenesis_newborn) — taking the most-adapted members
   with them.
4. The new species, seeded with the parent's best-adapted individuals, grows rapidly.
   The parent species, having lost its leading edge, declines.
5. The cycle repeats from the new species.

This creates an apparent species-replacement chain that is, mechanistically, the population
continuously adapting — but logged as thousands of speciation events rather than in-place
anagenesis. The anagenesis detector did not fire once across 30,000 weeks: either phenotype
drift never crossed the 0.93 threshold with 60-week persistence (possible in a single-fitness-peak
landscape where adaptation plateaus), or the constant cladogenesis is draining the divergence
signal before it accumulates.

Event breakdown: 1,782 cladogenesis_newborn, 237 cladogenesis_kmeans_subcluster, 0 anagenesis.
14,482 candidate clusters failed to reach promotion threshold (peaked at 1 member in most cases).
36,265 hybridization events occurred — roughly 1.2 per week per habitat.

### Evolution discovered a metabolic tolerance strategy — evidence of a richer fitness landscape

Mean food probability dropped from **0.877 at week 1 to 0.573 at week 30,000**, and water from
**0.884 to 0.541**, while total population grew nearly 10x. This is one of the most interesting
findings of the run.

Global trait shifts (population-weighted mean, week 1 → week 30,000):

| Trait | Week 1 | Week 30,000 | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| `metabolism` | 1.592 | 1.168 | **−0.424** |
| `water_efficiency` | 0.714 | 0.794 | +0.080 |
| `fecundity` | 5.93 | 7.02 | +1.09 |
| `base_predation_rate` | 0.0037 | 0.0042 | +0.0005 |
| `mutation_rate` | 0.0350 | 0.0302 | −0.0048 |
| `migration_likelihood` | 0.753 | 0.805 | +0.052 |
| `max_lifespan` | 302.8 | 305.7 | +2.9 |

The population converged on a **metabolic tolerance strategy**: rather than maintaining tight
alignment with the habitat vector, evolution discovered that drastically reducing the *cost* of
resource misses (metabolism −0.42, water_efficiency +0.08) combined with higher fecundity
(+1.09) is a viable and apparently superior long-run equilibrium.

This is not a failure of the simulation — it is evidence that the fitness landscape is not a
single Gaussian centered on the habitat vector. **Habitat alignment is one axis of fitness, but
it is not the only one.** Metabolism, water_efficiency, and fecundity are themselves under
genuine selective pressure, and together they define an alternative viable optimum: a low-cost,
high-reproduction generalist strategy that doesn't require maximal resource-finding ability.
The simulation found a local minimum in the cost surface that real evolutionary biology would
recognize — r-strategists thrive in variable or resource-scarce environments not by finding
resources more reliably, but by making scarcity cheap to survive.

What this points to is a tuning question, not a design flaw. The current cost parameters make
the metabolic-tolerance strategy *too* dominant — it completely displaces alignment pressure
over 1,200 generations. The fitness landscape likely needs a more nuanced cost surface where
both strategies are viable in different conditions: alignment-first in resource-stable habitats,
tolerance-first in variable ones. Concrete directions:

- Raise the baseline energy/hydration drain so that even a low-metabolism creature needs some
  minimum alignment to survive (a floor below which tolerance alone cannot compensate).
- Make the metabolic cost of very low alignment superlinear — missing food 50% of the time
  should be disproportionately more costly than missing it 20% of the time, creating a soft
  cliff that rewards alignment at the margins.
- These changes would produce a landscape with multiple inhabited optima rather than one
  winning strategy, which is the goal.

The rise in `base_predation_rate` (+0.0005) is a pleiotropic byproduct of rising fecundity —
these traits share loci, encoding a r/K trade-off. The drop in mutation_rate (−0.005) is
consistent with long-run stabilizing selection once a viable strategy has been found.

### k-means subcluster splits: leaky boundaries and rapid burnout

Of the 237 k-means split events, nearly all split-off species went extinct within a few hundred
to a few thousand weeks of their birth. The split fires when two sub-cluster centroids fall
below the `split_isolation_threshold` of 0.70 — which equals the mating floor
(`COMPATIBILITY_FLOOR`). This means centroids are just barely incompatible, but individual
members at the cluster margins may still have pairwise compatibility above 0.70 and continue
hybridizing across the split.

The observed consequence (verified in iron follower / venomous wriggler at week 1,410–1,950):
the smaller split-off species peaks at or near its birth population, then declines while the
parent recovers. The likely mechanism is that hybrid offspring from cross-cluster matings are
assigned to whichever centroid they match best — which tends to be the larger, more genetically
coherent parent. The split-off cluster loses effective replacement births and ages out.

**Recommendation:** Raise `split_isolation_threshold` to 0.75–0.78 to ensure detected splits
represent genuinely clean reproductive isolation, not marginal centroid separation where
individuals at the boundary are still compatible.

### No allopatric speciation: isolation_probability too low

Zero spontaneous route closures occurred across 30,000 weeks × 5 links = 150,000
link-weeks at p = 1e-5. The expected value was 1.5 closures, but the stochastic draw came up
empty. For future ring runs targeting allopatric speciation, raise `isolation_probability` to
~5e-5 (expected 7–8 closures over the run, enough to see genuine periods of isolation) or
accept that geographic isolation will be rare at this timescale.

### Cause of death: unresolvable from this run, tracking needed

`events_every = 0` means individual deaths were not logged. From trait trajectories we can
infer that starvation and dehydration dominate given the low food/water probabilities at late
run (0.57 and 0.54), but we cannot confirm cause of death or compare across species.
**Add per-cause death counts to `summary.json`** — at minimum, global counts of
starvation / dehydration / predation / old_age accumulated over the full run, ideally
broken down by habitat. This is cheap to maintain as a running counter without enabling
full event logging.

### Open questions and recommended next steps

1. **Harsher density penalty**: double or triple `PREDATION_ALPHA` to impose meaningful
   carrying capacity per habitat and prevent unbounded population growth.
2. **Cost surface tuning**: the metabolic-tolerance strategy completely dominated alignment
   over 1,200 generations, suggesting the fitness landscape needs rebalancing — not removal
   of the tolerance axis. Add a superlinear cost component to high miss-rates (so missing
   food 50% of the time is disproportionately worse than 20%), and raise the baseline drain
   so a floor of alignment is always required regardless of metabolic efficiency. Goal is
   a landscape where both strategies are viable in different habitat conditions, not one
   where tolerance universally outcompetes alignment.
3. **Cause-of-death counters in summary.json**: running totals per cause, per habitat, cheap
   to add to the simulation's weekly death processing.
4. **Higher isolation_probability**: 5e-5 to actually observe allopatric speciation from
   route closure in a 30k-week ring run.
5. **split_isolation_threshold raised to 0.75–0.78**: to ensure k-means splits represent
   clean reproductive isolation rather than marginal separation.
6. **Targeted re-run for cladogenesis verification**: a short run (~2,000 weeks) with
   `events_every = 50` around a known split event to test the hybrid-reabsorption theory
   directly by tracing newborn species assignments.

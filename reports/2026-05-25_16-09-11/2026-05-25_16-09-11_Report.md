# Isolated habitats from identical founding stock produce mechanically-grounded trait divergence and competitive exclusion to one winner per biome

---

## Experiment Summary

**Config:** `parallel_divergence.toml` (copy in this folder)
**Log dir:** `simulation_logs/2026-05-25_16-09-11/`
**Run date:** 2026-05-25

Five isolated habitats (Forest, Desert, Tundra, Wetlands, Plains) were each seeded with the **same three founding species** using `mirror_founding_population = true` — identical founding genomes with fresh per-creature noise, no habitat bias. No connections, no migration. The goal was to observe how independent lineages diverge from a common ancestor when subjected to different habitat selection vectors.

**Key non-defaults from the default config:**

| Parameter | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `mirror_founding_population` | `true` | Same founding genomes across all habitats |
| `creatures_per_species` | 60 | Larger founding populations for harsh-habitat viability |
| `weeks` | 30,000 | Long enough for ~500+ generations of selection |
| `stats_every` | 50 | Reduced logging cadence for speed |
| `events_every` | 0 | Disabled per-event logging for speed |
| `respeciate_every` | 50 | Matches logging cadence |
| `founding_habitat_bias` | 0.0 | Required for mirror mode; all habitats start from same genes |
| `isolation_probability` | 0.0 | No route isolation; habitats are permanently disconnected |

**Starting state:** 900 creatures total (5 habitats × 3 species × 60 creatures), 3 founding species:
*jade dweller*, *mystic striker*, *bristled climber*

---

## Report Summary

**Why this was interesting:** The mirrored founding design creates a controlled evolution experiment — all habitats start from literally the same genetics, so any divergence at the end is caused by selection, not founder effects. It also tests whether harsh habitats (Desert, Tundra) can sustain populations without adaptation headstarts.

**What was found:**
- Desert and Tundra went fully extinct. No founding bias meant random genomes weren't aligned to those harsh environment vectors; populations died before selection could act.
- 110 speciation events produced 113 total species over 30,000 weeks, but competitive exclusion left **one dominant species per surviving habitat** at the end.
- Mechanically-grounded traits (metabolism, water efficiency, lifespan, fecundity) show real, large divergences across habitats — all 17–185× above the sampling noise floor established by the identical founding condition.
- An unexpected burst of 10 anagenesis events (in-place lineage transformation) concentrated in two habitat-specific clusters, suggesting rapid morphological turnover in established lineages.

**Takeaway:** The dot-product resource geometry creates strong, consistent directional selection. Given enough time, one lineage wins per habitat through competitive exclusion. The mechanically-active traits show clean r/K life-history divergence across habitats. Many other traits appear to diverge but are epiphenomenal — they changed because their gene loci were dragged along by selection on the resource-alignment genes, not because of any direct habitat interaction.

## Report

### Extinction events

Desert and Tundra both collapsed to zero population before meaningful adaptation could occur. With `founding_habitat_bias = 0.0`, all creatures start with random genomes that have ~50% food/water probability in any habitat. The Desert and Tundra habitat vectors impose particularly low survival on random gene configurations; the populations died faster than selection could improve alignment. This is a design finding: **harsh habitats require either a founding bias or migration from adapted neighbors to sustain a population.**

### Speciation timeline

| Window | Events | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| wk 0–5,000 | 23 | Fastest phase; populations differentiating across habitats |
| wk 5,000–10,000 | 22 | Continued high rate; burst of 10 anagenesis events in this window |
| wk 10,000–15,000 | 17 | Rate slowing as niches fill |
| wk 15,000–20,000 | 19 | Moderate; some rebound |
| wk 20,000–25,000 | 10 | Slowest phase; dominant species consolidating |
| wk 25,000–30,000 | 19 | Late burst; ongoing diversification within surviving habitats |

**Total:** 110 confirmed speciation events (100 cladogenesis, 10 anagenesis)
**Failed attempts:** 1,214 — a ~92% failure rate, reflecting the two-stage candidate gate filtering transient lineages
**Hybridization events:** 2,294 across the full run

First speciation: week 867 (*jade dweller* → *skeletal creeper*)
All three founding species were still alive at this point.

### Anagenesis events

Ten in-place lineage transformations occurred, all concentrated in two bursts:

- **Weeks 6,050–6,950:** Six successive transformations from *fractured borer* (Forest). This lineage transformed six times in 900 weeks — rapid morphological turnover while remaining a single interbreeding population.
- **Weeks 11,400–11,550:** Two transformations from *magnetic slider* (Wetlands), plus two more later.

No anagenesis occurred in Plains. This suggests the Forest and Wetlands habitat vectors created steeper, more persistent directional selection pressure on the phenotype axis — enough to repeatedly cross the 0.93 threshold over the 60-week persistence gate.

### Surviving species at week 30,000

| Species | Habitat | Population | Generations |
|---|---|---|---|
| *blazing swimmer* | Forest | 925 | ~522 |
| *ochre chaser* | Plains | 864 | ~531 |
| *wandering caller* | Wetlands | 596 | ~537 |

All three are descended from the original three founding species through multiple speciation and extinction events. None of the original three founding species (*jade dweller*, *mystic striker*, *bristled climber*) survived to week 30,000.

### Trait divergence analysis

**Noise baseline:** Week 1 same-species cross-habitat spread was 0.001–0.003 (pure sampling noise from the mirrored founding condition). All reported divergences are measured against this floor.

**Important caveat:** Most creature traits do not directly interact with the simulation mechanics. Selection pressure operates through gene-vector alignment to the habitat vector (determining food/water probability). Traits that are *not* wired into mechanics diverge because their gene loci are pleiotropically entangled with loci that do affect survival — not because of any direct habitat–trait relationship. Only the mechanically-active traits below support causal interpretation.

#### Mechanically-active trait divergences

These traits directly affect survival or reproduction in the simulation:

| Trait | Forest | Wetlands | Plains | Spread | Signal/Noise | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Metabolism** (0.5–2.0) | **1.380** | 1.607 | 1.603 | 0.227 | 99× | Forest: energy-conservative in patchy resource environment; Plains/Wetlands: high-throughput where resources more reliable |
| **Water efficiency** (0–1) | 0.719 | 0.648 | **0.786** | 0.068 | 25× | Plains slightly highest; Wetlands lowest — though functional spread is modest |
| **Lifespan** (40–400 wk) | 325 | **347** | 295 | 52 wk | 100× | r/K split: Plains fast-living (295 wk), Wetlands slow-living (347 wk) |
| **Fecundity** (1–8) | 6.945 | 6.945 | **7.053** | 0.109 | 6× | Plains highest; modest but real signal |

**The clearest mechanically-supported story — r/K life history split:**

Plains *ochre chaser*: shorter lifespan (295 wk), highest fecundity (7.05), fast metabolism (1.60) → r-selected: many offspring, burn fast, die young.

Wetlands *wandering caller*: longest lifespan (347 wk), largest body size, slower fecundity, fast metabolism (1.61) → K-selected: fewer offspring, live long, invest heavily.

Forest *blazing swimmer*: lowest metabolism (1.38) — the most striking divergence. Dense forest with patchy, competitive resources selected for energy conservation over throughput. Lower metabolism means lower energy cost when food is missed, which is a survival advantage in an environment where food probability is inconsistent.

#### Non-mechanically-active traits (divergence is real but epiphenomenal)

The following traits diverged significantly above the noise floor but their divergence reflects gene-pool movement in the habitat's direction in 500-dim space — not any direct habitat environment property:

| Trait | Signal/Noise | Highest | Lowest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intelligence | 185× | Plains (0.817) | Wetlands (0.687) |
| Stress tolerance | 119× | Wetlands (0.830) | Plains (0.735) |
| Cold tolerance | 118× | Plains (0.829) | Wetlands (0.711) |
| Nocturnal tendency | 38× | Forest (0.811) | Plains (0.677) |
| Size | 40× | Wetlands (0.898) | Forest (0.783) |

These are real evolutionary changes — not noise — but the trait labels don't tell us *why* selection moved in that direction. They moved because they share gene loci with whatever the habitat vector was selecting for.

### Open questions from this run

1. Would Desert and Tundra survive with `founding_habitat_bias = 0.3`? Or with migration connections from an adapted neighboring habitat?
2. The burst of 6 anagenesis events from Forest in weeks 6,050–6,950 is striking — is this a property of the Forest habitat vector geometry, or a seeding coincidence? Worth repeating with different seeds.
3. Why does Plains have no anagenesis events despite being a strong selective environment? Is its habitat vector already close to the founding genome's gene-space direction, making phenotype drift slower?
4. Competitive exclusion to one species per habitat happened reliably — is this an inevitable outcome of the resource geometry, or does it depend on initial diversity and run length?
